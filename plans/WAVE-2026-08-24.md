# Wave plan — 2026-08-24

Five workstreams, three waves, ordered by dependency. **A wave starts only once
every workstream it depends on has reported back.** Each stream owns disjoint
files; if you need a change outside your "owns" list, report it rather than
making it.

Written after `BP12` closed (`PLAN.md` §2.10). Two things frame everything here:
**no engine beats pasting the keyframe**, and **the paper deadline is
September 30** — the platform plus these controlled negative results plus BP13's
headroom number is already a submittable paper, and the generator working is an
improvement on that, not a precondition for it.

## Wave 1 — start now, all three in parallel

| Stream | Brief | Where | GPU? |
|---|---|---|---|

| **B — headroom** | `BP13-motivating-headroom.md` | this repo + paper repo | no |

**Why these three together.** They share no files, none needs a GPU campaign,
and each removes a different blocker: A restores the regression signal, B decides
whether the premise is worth GPU at all, C builds the instrument every later
question needs.

**Ownership, so they cannot collide:**

- A: `.github/workflows/**`, `pytest.ini`, `config/*.yaml`, the named-weight
  invariant, the weight resolver.
- B: `experiments/headroom/**`, and `sections/problem.tex` in the **paper repo**
  (separate git repo at `67a9ea6275d3d9785ce57026/`, own `AGENTS.md`, commit
  there).
- C: `src/components/metrics/**`, `tests/invariants/test_metric_calibration.py`.

**B is the one that matters most.** It is the only stream whose result can
change what the project does: a player bounding box is ~1% of a 4K frame, and if
a conventional encoder spends 3% of its bits on the players there is no prize
here regardless of how good a generator becomes. It needs ffmpeg and hours, not
GPUs and days. Nothing else should consume GPU time before that number exists.

## Wave 3 — decided by Wave 1's result, not scheduled now

What happens here is a **fork on B's number**, and it should be written down
before B runs so the decision is not made by whoever likes the answer:

- **FG headroom ≥ 25%** — the premise is strong. Spend GPU: retrain the
  ControlNets on the *right task* (see below), and evaluate with C's instrument.
- **10–25%** — real but modest. The background and residual carry the paper;
  generator work becomes an improvement track, not the headline.
- **< 10%** — the premise is weak. Redirect now, in time to matter, and the
  paper becomes the platform, the ablation lattice, and an honest account of why
  object-centric generative coding does not pay on this content. That is still a
  paper, and a more useful one than a weak positive.

`BP14-training-stop-rule.md` lands **before any training run** in any branch of
that fork. The last one burned 14 GPU hours on a series flat from epoch 1.

The Wave-3 work itself is specified in **`BP19-conditioning-architecture.md`**.

## What the retraining question actually looks like

Recorded here so Wave 3 does not restart the analysis from scratch. Three
appearance channels exist on paper; their real status differs:

| Channel | Registered as | Actual status |
|---|---|---|
| **Text / caption** | `pose-controlnet` (alias `caption-controlnet`) | **Trained, never driven.** 114 per-track BLIP captions exist, 46% name a kit colour, and inference hardcodes the generic fallback. This is `BP17`, and it needs no training. |
| **Keyframe / reference image** | `pose-ref-controlnet` | Trained, measured, **failed and understood**. The reference was painted into the *control* image, and the control branch is trained to read structure (§2.4). Do not repeat that recipe. |
| **Latent / image embedding** | `ip-adapter-controlnet` declares `appearance:image-embedding` | **Declared, not trained.** The checkpoint is a mislabelled segmentation ControlNet (§2.3), which is why its appearance dependence is the lowest on the roster. A real IP-Adapter arm has never been trained. |

So the honest summary is: of three appearance pathways, one is off, one failed
for a known architectural reason, and one was never actually built.

**The shared-backbone idea is sound, has a name, and now has a brief.**
Converting each condition type through its own head into a common representation
that one backbone consumes is what **Uni-ControlNet** does — a local adapter for
structural conditions plus a global adapter for image embeddings, over a single
SD-1.5 backbone — and what UniControl and Composer do differently. It fits our
stack and §6.2's "fixed backbone, variable conditioning".

All of it — a real IP-Adapter arm, retraining on the coding task, the shared
backbone, and the ReferenceNet extension deferred from `BP12` — is
**`BP19-conditioning-architecture.md`**. That brief is **gated** on B (headroom),
`BP14` (stop rule), C (instrument) and D (caption channel), and says so at the
top. It is the only stream that spends real GPU time, and it does not start on
its own initiative.

It also names the cheapest thing nobody has done: **`multi-controlnet` is
registered with pose and mask as separate conditions and has never been
measured** — the existing multi-condition arm, and a free probe of the shared
conditioning idea.

## Reporting back

Each stream reports: what it did, what it measured with n and a standard error,
what it did **not** do, and anything it found outside its own files. Use the
`verify-measurement` skill before reporting any number, and
`python -m experiments.probe.report <run-dir>` for anything roster-shaped.

**The rule that earned its place today: a control needs its own null.** If your
stream produces a ranking, run the degenerate arm through it — the paste, the
passthrough, the empty model — *before* reading the ranking.
