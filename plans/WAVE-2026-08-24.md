## Wave 3 — the fork is decided: build the generator

`BP20` reported (`PLAN.md` §2.14). On real 4K the players are **~1% of pixels
and 17–24% of the bitrate**, a 15–47× concentration. That is the top of the
"modest" band against AVC and comfortably inside it against VVC, so:

**The premise holds and generator work is justified.** `BP19` is unblocked on
this gate. Two conditions come with it, and both change what `BP19` should
target:

1. **Report the whole ladder and name VVC as the exception.** AVC, HEVC and AV1
   agree at 0.20–0.26; VVC sits ~0.077 lower, and that step is the only thing
   that repeats cleanly on both clips. It is **not** a trend across codec
   generations — that reading was over-reading n=2 — and VVC also ran at
   QP 47 rather than 48, a confound that must be ruled out before the codec is
   blamed.
2. **The background stays in, framed honestly.** A panorama plate plus
   homographies saves **34–69%** of the background bitrate (best on VVC). Not
   the 17.4× a synthetic JPEG comparison suggested, but a substantial second
   half of the motivating example, and it *improves* with codec strength where
   the foreground does not.

Still gating `BP19`: `BP14` (a stop rule) before any training run, and `BP18`'s
instrument (done) for evaluating whatever comes out.

`BP14-training-stop-rule.md` lands **before any training run** in any branch of
that fork. `BP19-conditioning-architecture.md` is the Wave-3 work itself and is
gated on A, `BP14`, `BP18` (done) and `BP17`.

## What Wave 1 actually established

Worth carrying, because two of the three results are negative and easy to lose:

- **No engine beats pasting the keyframe** (§2.10), at 2.5σ–10.6σ, and the best
  of the eight is a non-generative upscaler. The cross-appearance test is
  withdrawn as a test of appearance use — a paste tops it.
- **An identity instrument now exists** (§2.12) and is calibrated on ground-truth
  pairs at 17.1σ. `reid` for identity, `palette` as its cross-check, and an
  `IdentityScale` so a score is quoted between its measured anchors rather than
  against an imagined zero.
- **One appearance channel was never switched on** (§2.11): the ControlNets were
  trained with per-track captions naming kit colour, and inference hardcodes a
  generic fallback. That is stream C.

## Reporting back

Each stream reports: what it did, what it did **not** do, every number with n and
a standard error and the instrument's range beside it, and anything found
outside its own files. Use `verify-measurement` before reporting any number.

**Two rules earned this week, both the hard way:**

- **A control needs its own null.** If your stream produces a ranking, run the
  degenerate arm through it — the paste, the passthrough, the empty model —
  before reading the ranking.
- **Measure the thing, not a model of the thing.** A harness that runs, passes
  its tests, writes its bounds first and then encodes a synthetic rectangle has
  verified the harness, not the claim. Name your input in the first line of the
  report.
