# Prospective paired one- and three-step cost pilot

This is a new, separately budgeted experiment, not a replay of failed job `20261105T222814Z-8eeacee2`. That job remains failed on its combined forecast and remaining-time gate. Its one-step qualification informed this design; it has not established quality or authorized 30-step generation. The cross-campaign prototype is preserved on its separate branch and is not included here.

The actual same-campaign one-step smoke and three-step full stage use the original `cost_run`, `cost_validate`, `audit_pair`, packet and generator, source guard, isolation, model, inputs and clock policy. Only `stage_inputs` changes, and only an exact `w8-paired-cost-800-v1` profile admits a 800-second campaign. Legacy specifications retain the 600-second maximum and exact original 15 inputs. The profile pins the 260-second smoke, 500-second full, 60-second validator, steps 1 and 3, CPU/wall caps and original arguments. The original validator independently checks the new actual one-step output and runs its unchanged 1.5 whole-stage forecast and remaining-time gate; the fleet also requires the declared 500 seconds remain before dispatching full. Any failure stops without retry. The draft deadline is a placeholder and must be refreshed after review.

The prior one-step command took 130.837 seconds, while its whole supervisor lifetime of 171.299 seconds bounds that prior smoke measurement including validation. Its 32.728-second sampling and materialization floor give the same 1.5 three-step forecast of 356.246 seconds. A deliberately conservative planning sum is 171.299 + 356.246 + 99.249 + 60 = 686.794 seconds against 800: the 99.249 is the prior forecast for two setup/cleanup intervals, and the extra 60 is a reserve. The first supervisor lifetime already includes its first setup and validator, so adding two setup intervals and this reserve double-counts some elapsed time; this makes the sum conservative, but it is a model from one prior run, not a bound or guarantee for a future run. The new actual smoke and remaining-time gate determine whether full runs.

The new generator CPU cap is 290 seconds and worker wall cap is 300. Applying 1.5 to prior generator wall plus two observed sampling intervals gives about 263.24 seconds; applying it to prior generator CPU plus two sampling CPU intervals gives about 262.14 seconds. These are projections, not runtime guarantees. The unchanged cost-run CPU calculation reserves `2 × (30 parent + 100 packet + 290 generator) + 60 validator + 100 external = 1,000` seconds. A separate proposed 100 CPU seconds is held for independent post-run audit; the proposed inclusive ceiling is 900 GPU / 1,100 CPU seconds, including 800 GPU campaign and 100 seconds GPU reserve. No release or resource reservation follows from this draft.

Before any request, independently review the source, exact full archive and normalized spec, preserve the original 15 input hashes, prove the route can carry the full snapshot, confirm all fleet jobs/claims and compatible host readiness, select a fresh deadline, and record budgets. A successful three-step run still needs independent `audit_pair`, terminal/provenance and protected-policy review. Its forecast is advisory and cannot automatically launch a 30-step or quality run.
