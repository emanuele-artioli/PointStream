# Experiments: additional sport domain screen

### 2026-11-15 — read-only screen

**Question.** Is one extra sport worth a cheap falsifiable test alongside tennis and egocentric hand–object video? This is a source and feasibility screen, not experimental evidence or authorization for a run. No data were downloaded, decoded, trained on, or run on GPUs. The local checkout has no `Datasets` link, so the server's manifests and any unlisted historic footage were not independently inspected. The documented inventory in [resources](../resources.md#datasets) lists racket and egocentric datasets, not the three candidates; absence from that table does not prove absence on the server. Check existing manifests and old GenStream inputs read-only before acquiring anything.

**Ranking, conditional on usable RGB and rights.** (1) Figure skating if existing GenStream RGB is usable: strongest scientific complement per likely actor, and direct continuity with GenStream; a moving broadcast camera and image detail make the real background rate a meaningful countertest. (2) Volleyball: SportsMOT is a credible backup with 25 fps source footage and a many-person/occlusion contrast; multiple people increase costs and usable RGB access and masks remain unresolved. (3) Soccer: high generality and difficult tiny-ball/many-player stress test, but available sources vary: SoccerNet broadcast footage is gated, while SoccerTrack v2 offers a different panoramic view; distant players and ball make attribution and annotation expensive. This ranking is a hypothesis, not a measured gain. A larger actor in the image is not itself a bitrate win: foreground representation, masks, side information, compositing, and background motion all consume bits. Skating's low person count helps cost but tracking shots can make the background expensive.

**Figure skating sources and limits.** [GenStream](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18634) partially validates on Olympic skating, transmitting skeletons and viewpoint with a static 3D background; its [paper](https://athena.itec.aau.at/wp-content/uploads/sites/12/2025/08/41_GenStream_Semantic_Streamin.pdf) describes a manually constructed rink model. Its continuous-stream comparison does not establish PointStream end-to-end savings with a normally coded background. Do not assume a known or reconstructed 3D scene at the client. The authors' [FineFS release](https://github.com/yanliji/FineFS-dataset) describes 1,167 RGB MP4 single-athlete programs (46.3 GB), estimated skeletons and action labels; native cadence, actual source rights, access terms, masks, camera cuts and source independence need verification before use. Estimated skeletons are not ground truth masks. The [FSD documentation](https://github.com/PaddlePaddle/PaddleVideo/blob/develop/docs/en/dataset/fsd.md) states its video was standardized to 30 fps and its release excludes RGB for copyright, so those skeleton arrays cannot establish codec performance. Prior skating footage, if available, may be cheaper but provenance, rights, source splits and training overlap must be established.

**Volleyball.** [SportsMOT](https://github.com/MCG-NJU/SportsMOT) describes 240 basketball, football and volleyball clips cut from official YouTube recordings selected at 720p and 25 fps, without shot changes; volleyball averages 11.2 labelled player boxes per frame. Its [dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MCG-NJU/SportsMOT) describes MOT-format JPEG sequences, player boxes and IDs (including estimated boxes for partly occluded players), not masks or ball labels; the card lists CC BY-NC 4.0 metadata. The viewer exposes split IDs, so RGB availability through that specific endpoint remains unverified. Source cadence is the selected recording cadence, not proof of original capture cadence. Verify actual media, provenance and rights before acquisition. The older The [original dataset repository](https://github.com/mostafa-saad/deep-activity-rec) describes 55 videos, 4,830 labelled frames, player boxes and activity labels, and 41-image windows around annotated frames; 8 videos are 1080p and the rest 720p. It does not establish native video cadence, dense masks, ball trajectories, or full-length continuous source video for coding; source-video and redistribution rights require checking. Numerous players, net crossings and occlusion make a useful stress case, but small/blurred balls and moving cameras must be measured on actual clips, not assumed from category.

**Soccer.** [SoccerTrack v2](https://atomscott.github.io/SoccerTrack-v2/) describes ten full 4K panoramic university matches (~900 minutes) with per-frame pitch coordinates and player identities and ball-action timestamps; its [data licence](https://github.com/AtomScott/SoccerTrack-v2/blob/main/LICENSE-DATA) is CC BY 4.0. The site lists open access, but accessible RGB files, exact source cadence and any sample-specific restrictions have not been independently verified. Panoramic full-pitch footage is a different camera regime from broadcast. [SoccerNet's data page](https://www.soccer-net.org/data) describes broadcast MKV at 25 fps and 720p or 224p, and tracking labels for players, referees and ball on main-camera clips. Its 2 fps features are not a native-cadence video substitute. The [official raw-video card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SoccerNet/SoccerNet_raw_HQ) says raw media are gated and notes third-party broadcast rights; verify access and allowed usage. Tracking boxes are not segmentation masks. Many small athletes, tiny ball, occlusion, shot changes and pans can make both representation and scoring costly; inspect actual eligible samples before claiming their prevalence.

**One proposed pilot after core tennis, not yet funded: figure skating, conditional on a usable existing GenStream source.** First check existing rights-cleared continuous RGB and source provenance, cadence and scene cuts. If none is usable, stop until an accessible rights-cleared source is identified; do not substitute skeleton-only data or upsampled frames. Sample a few independent programs with held-out skaters and varied camera motion, preselecting short continuous native-cadence segments; document any training overlap and report a clean subset. Independently annotated actor masks or a clearly scoped full-frame metric are necessary; predicted poses are not labels. Record counts, foreground coverage, occlusion and camera motion without selecting only favorable shots.

Hypothesis: in single-skater continuous shots, a reusable actor representation can improve the project’s foreground-weighted rate–quality target, with a player-only claim, when the *actual* coded native background, foreground, masks, metadata and all initialization/amortized assets are charged. Competing explanation: background motion, actor appearance, occlusion, model and side-information costs erase any advantage, even for a large skater. First design a demonstrably dominating oracle for the exact candidate and operating point; if its bound cannot dominate the gated implementation, validate or revise it before using it as a gate. Use actual paid-in-bits coded background, no assumed client 3D world, and include all transmitted bytes. Compare against an achievable SVT-AV1 envelope and the project's neural baseline, using BD-rate with PCHIP over overlapping quality ranges for the project’s 70/30 foreground/background quality target, and report per-source full-frame and independently labelled foreground/background quality and costs. Where curves do not overlap report better/worse at every rate. Include temporal and semantic checks, and count all people or explicitly restrict claims to single-skater shots. A generic or tennis-adapted model might transfer: no zero-shot failure is established here.

Decision: continue only if a valid bound and subsequent real-codec pilot support a rate advantage at comparable 70/30 weighted quality, without unacceptable actor/temporal errors, on more than one independent source; otherwise report the failure and narrow to the camera/shot conditions where it held before proposing another test. Full-frame results and background costs are reported, not an additional win gate; claims remain player-only. The oracle is a feasibility bound, not a codec result. Before any computation, freeze quantitative quality/error thresholds, sample selection, encoder settings and a decision rule in a run card; set separate CPU wall-time and GPU-hour budgets and per-stage deadlines no longer than 45 minutes. These budgets, source availability, rights, annotation cost and a validated dominating oracle are unresolved; no GPU time is authorized by this screen. Follow the project smoke, fleet, provenance and resource-budget protocol for any future stage.
