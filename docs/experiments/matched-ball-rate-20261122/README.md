# Matched-rate tracked-ball ablation

Question: at the complete 4.8-second delivery rate of the existing plain AV1 CRF48 control (182,055 bytes, 303.425 kbps), what ball quality and whole-video quality are traded when a lower-rate native base carries the existing 195-position tracked-pixel sidecar? Hypothesis: ball-crop DISTS improves while whole-video fidelity decreases only modestly. The competing explanation is that the 19,114-byte sidecar costs more background fidelity than its ball improvement justifies. The mode here sends real source tiles; it is distinct from the rendered streak used by the game9/Clip5 detection study.

Use the original 240-frame 50 Hz Nadal–Kyrgios development shot, 960×544 SVT-AV1 preset 4 with the same encoded source YUV, output at 1920×1080, same sender track and tile stream. Charge the actual complete native-only packet and sidecar including all headers; plain AV1 charges the complete IVF. Freeze CRF50 as a candidate before its full encode, informed by the existing CRF48 and CRF60 rates. Run one 24-frame encoder/decode smoke and check the resulting frame count and tool/source identity. A smoke cannot bind a 240-frame sidecar. If the actual full CRF50 packet total falls within 3% of the 182,055-byte target, retain it as an approximately matched operating point and measure the exact rates; otherwise try one adjacent CRF, chosen by whether the packet is above or below budget. Keep every tried rate and do not extrapolate without reporting its support. Use at most 240 local CPU seconds, 180 elapsed seconds, with no GPU. Report all-inclusive child CPU time and preserve outputs under a fresh directory. If the candidate does not fit within 3% after one correction, stop and keep the bracketing results.

At the selected point, independently decode the actual packets, render all 240 frames with the same implementation, and score against the source: whole-frame PSNR over the 12 source-reviewed ticks on native-sized RGB (including the ball), plus the existing player masks and ball-crop DISTS on those exact ticks, with rates alongside every result. These 12 visible ticks are development evidence, not a recall estimate. The 240-frame receiver check must independently reconstruct each tile placement and verify outside-patch pixels. Never use the display video as the metric input. Compute an all-frame whole-luma PSNR check on the native 960×544 base as a second way to describe the cost of allocating bits to the sidecar; it excludes the pasted ball by construction.

For ball detection, qualify an image-only judge distinct from the sender and labels. Freeze its checkpoint, confidence threshold, geometry, tolerance and postprocessing before scoring any comparison. Have independent source-only annotations for every frame or a prespecified representative sample, including absent and uncertain frames. Report detected visible frames within 10 pixels, localization tolerance sensitivity, false positives per absent frame, numerator and denominator, and paired frame outcomes. Judge all actual decoded frames at the same total-rate settings. Report sender misses, false placements and visible frames separately; detected pixels must not be inferred from the transmitted track. The same judge must be checked on clean source, with its training/exposure audited. Compare matched-quality plain and ROI codec curves and measure whole-frame costs. Do not claim human visibility from a detector alone.

The earlier game9/Clip5 rendered-streak study observed WASB 5 to 229 hits out of 261 visible frames at SVT-AV1 CRF62, and BallTrack 7 to 229. False positives on 30 absent frames rose from 3 to 5 for both judges. This was 63.06 versus 64.08 kbps and is not a matched-total-rate experiment. ## Development outcome

The real candidate met the 3% byte rule on the first try. The 24-frame smoke took 7.88 process-tree CPU seconds. The full 240-frame encode and packet construction took 59.78 process-tree CPU seconds. Both rates below charge their complete packets and the candidate is 0.094% higher in bytes. The existing scorer used 11.14 CPU seconds after its zero-distance identity smoke and admitted all 12 ticks. The RGB and luma comparison process did not record its CPU usage, so total CPU consumption cannot be established from these receipts. No GPU was used.

| Decode | kbps | Player 1 DISTS | Player 2 DISTS | Ball crop DISTS | Whole-frame RGB PSNR, 12 ticks |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Plain AV1 CRF48 | 303.425 | 0.2294 | 0.2255 | 0.1969 | 32.1690 dB |
| AV1 CRF50 plus tracked source tiles | 303.710 | 0.2341 | 0.2383 | 0.1614 | 32.0652 dB |

Thus ball-crop DISTS falls 0.0355, and whole-frame RGB PSNR falls 0.1038 dB on the 12 reviewed ticks. The separate 240-frame native base luma PSNR falls from 41.3317 to 40.9259 dB. This latter number excludes the tile composite. The ball-only composite uses a frozen sender track, so it leaves 45 frames without an added tile. Only 12 visible ticks are independently labeled. Exact native resolution, source images, masks and metric weights match the [earlier plain-control score](../clean-court-regional-screen-20261118/control-scores.json). Scoring used decoded lossless RGB, not a display video. The complete receiver matched an independent per-pixel tile composition on all 240 ticks.

The result uses one opened rally and a small human-labeled subset. It shows the requested rate tradeoff as a development observation. It does not supply a detector recall, formal user study, unseen-match estimate, or the matched-rate neural and ROI comparisons.

## Paired detection preparation

Two concrete comparisons are registered. The tracked-pixel comparison above is Nadal–Kyrgios `000@120`, 240 frames at 50 Hz, plain 303.425 kbps versus tracked tiles 303.710 kbps. The low-rate renderer comparison is TrackNet `game9/Clip5`, 292 frames at 30 Hz. Its archived same-CRF62 baseline and fitted streak cost 63.0575 and 64.0849 kbps. The next test must encode a slightly lower-rate base and bind the fitted streak while targeting a complete total of 63.0575 kbps, with every byte charged. For this new low-rate trial tighten the matching criterion to within 0.5% of the baseline and require a genuinely reduced base allocation. The historical +1.63% pair remains the exploratory comparison. If integer CRF steps are too coarse, use the closest two feasible base bit budgets to bracket the comparison, report each actual rate and gap, and reserve any matched-rate inference for the overlap. Padding cannot substitute for reallocation. Refit at the selected operating point and rejudge both actual decodes, including missed tracks and absence frames. Retain the original 5/229 result as a higher-rate reference. Compare whole-frame PSNR and perceptual quality at matched rates as well as visible recall.

The historical judge uses the frozen `wasb-step1` production pipeline and its complete checkpoint and config-tree digests recorded in the [source provenance](../k5d-inputs/svt-study4282-64160888-confirmation292-root-acceptance-20261113.json). Retain the existing configured postprocessor and tracker thresholds exactly, with their configuration contents and code-tree hashes recorded with every run. The clean-source, baseline, and candidate inference calls receive image arrays only. Labels and sender positions are held outside their worker; independent output is joined by ordered frame index after inference. Source frames, detector model, threshold policy and pixel geometry must match the declared input hashes. Fixed localization success is within 10 native source pixels, with 5 pixels reported as sensitivity. Do not tune thresholds on the evaluated outputs. WASB and the sender BallTrack have different checkpoint hashes, but separate weights alone do not prove absence of training-set overlap or data leakage. `game9/Clip5` is a previously opened calibration clip. The sender's model, judge model and TrackNet labels have their existing documented exposure limitations.

The [paired scoring script](paired_detection.py) accepts a JSON object with `source_sha256`, `label_file_sha256`, `judge_checkpoint_sha256`, `judge_config_sha256`, `sender_checkpoint_sha256`, `baseline_decode_sha256`, `candidate_decode_sha256`, `confidence_policy`, `judge_input` set to `decoded_pixels_only`, `coordinate_space` set to `source_native_pixels`, integer `width`, `height`, and three equal-length arrays `labels`, `baseline`, `candidate`. Each label is `{frame, kind, x, y}`, where kinds 1, 0, and -1 mean visible, absent, and uncertain. Each prediction is `{frame, detected, x, y}`. `label_file_sha256` hashes canonical UTF-8 JSON of the label array with sorted keys, compact separators and no trailing newline. The script validates ordered exact coverage, finite native coordinates, unequal judge/sender checkpoint hashes, distinct declared decoded-stream hashes and the label-array hash. It reports paired hit cells at both tolerances and false-alarm transitions on absent frames. Declared hashes and the decoded-pixels-only field are an input contract, not proof of worker isolation, actual file identities, confidence configuration or training history. A production wrapper must independently verify those facts before invoking it.
