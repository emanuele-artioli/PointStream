# Matched-base player and ball preview

For the 240-frame development rally, make two local display videos with the same decoded CRF 48 960×544 base and identical generated players. Apply the paid 32×32 ball tile to one video at the 195 registered ball positions. Compare the two outputs at every tick, including the 45 ticks without a placement. The ball sidecar adds 19,114 bytes to the 552,516-byte player carrier and receiver manifest, a 3.46% increase. The complete byte bills are 920.86 kbps and 952.72 kbps over 4.8 seconds. These cold-start rates include the full references.

The base is a conventional encode of the original video, including original players, rackets, and ball. Thus the left panel also shows the conventionally encoded ball. The right panel applies a separate paid ball tile in 195 frames. In the remaining 45 frames it retains the same ball pixels as the left panel. The reconstructed player layers cover only their masks. These previews are offline compositions of already audited receiver output, and the display H.264 files are separate from the paid wire data. They illustrate the ball-channel effect with the same underlying material, rather than an equal-rate quality comparison against a codec.

`make_preview.py` checks the source carrier and ball packet hashes, decoded base hash, generations and masks, placing exactly the authenticated receiver outputs in both variants. It checks that the ball changes no pixels outside its tile and records per-frame hashes before and after placement. macOS OpenCV interpolation is numerically different from the Linux runtime used for the earlier preview, so this pair was recomposed consistently on macOS from the same authenticated inputs. The hashes are recorded in the private receipt. The full composition and preview encodes used 17.46 CPU seconds after a successful eight-frame smoke, with no GPU. ffprobe verified 240 frames at 1920×1080 and 50 fps for each and 240 frames at 3840×1080 for the labeled combined view.
