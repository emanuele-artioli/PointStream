# How much independent tennis evidence counts?

The five SHA-identified original clip collections contain 159 scene files in five matches (Alcaraz–Hurkacz 21, Djokovic–Federer 23, Djokovic–Zverev 8, Medvedev–Struff 41 and Rublev–Auger-Aliassime 66). Nadal–Kyrgios adds an independent full-match source. Another archive label, Federer–Djokovic, contains 653 scene IDs each at nine AV1 operating points. Its encodes do not establish a seventh distinct original event. The former W7 feasibility set has nine windows from three of the above matches. Reusing an W7 window can replicate pipeline behavior without adding a held-out event.

The annotated RacketVision tennis inventory contains 431 clips and 150,399 frames; 21,544 frames have a ball annotation and 20,066 of those are visible. The official splits contain 350 training, 38 validation and 43 test clips. There are 7,395 racket-labeled frames and no player masks in this manifest. The 43 test match IDs identify distinct sequences; footage provenance must establish how many tournament matches or different players they represent. Do not count 43 verified independent broadcast events or call training clips external tests. The frozen first 12 test IDs and the assignment of the pre-existing Shanghai example are recorded in [the experiment card](README.md).

For the **complete human-viewing receiver**, report independent events, source shots, correctly routed ticks, and object-label coverage separately. A complete 240-frame display with a 120-frame object mode and 120 conventional frames is useful scene-selective coverage and must be broken out from a 240-frame all-object display. Predeclared gates and fallback remain in the denominator even if the renderer fails. Report training exposure for each player reference, common generator and match adapter. One player or many frames from one rally are repeated measures.

A practical next headline cohort is **all six verified events where raw footage qualifies, with three source-selected shots per event** and a separately identified, genuinely held-out stratum. If a seventh original event is verified, include it under the same rules. Freeze the event list and shot order before opening quality. Select the first three eligible shots under a fixed seeded order within each event, spanning separated points where timestamps permit; account separately for ineligible input shots, missing poses and camera cutaways. For an all-playback user claim, score fallbacks in place and include their duration and bytes. Twelve uniformly spaced objective sample instants per shot are a budgeted start for human source labels. Freeze them independently of reconstruction, count every visible object and provide separately scored active player, ball, background and complete-frame results at physical total rates. Multiple held-out events matter more than additional frames in either development match.

Use the match as the primary aggregation unit and display every per-match effect with its own shot count. Show the mean of match means, its leave-one-match-out range, and distributions of paired shot effects. If resampling, resample matches and then shots while preserving matched method comparisons. Six events give limited precision: even six concordant signs yield a two-sided exact sign-test probability of 0.03125 under independent, exchangeable event signs; five concordant signs yield 0.0625. This arithmetic is illustrative and cannot turn training-overlap matches into independent confirmation. Two full reconstructions are useful cases, but cannot support a population-wide performance guarantee. No particular sample count assures an acceptance decision.

For the **machine-facing ball stream**, the native external TEST clips and official ball labels directly test delivery and detection. Preserve each selected clip in the denominator, report visible/absent coverage, per-clip outcomes, and paired point estimates across verified match IDs; avoid frame-level confidence limits that assume independent video frames. Additional external scenes support this endpoint only. A claim covering the whole foreground across a population requires paired source-only labels and competing full outputs on the same footage.
