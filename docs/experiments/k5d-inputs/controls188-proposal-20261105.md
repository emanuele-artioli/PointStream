# Prospective 188-frame ball sensitivity controls

Run the five frozen SVT-62 control arms on the already accepted `cc450baf` Clip3 188-frame stream: native, original streak, zero-exposure disk, wrong horizontal position (+64 pixels), and white brightness. The original pooled RGB PSNR guard, labels, whole-frame judge inputs, rendering, per-frame metrics, strict stream-byte accounting and scope are unchanged. The two synthetic diagnostic arms make no coding-rate claim. This is a sensitivity measurement, not a new encoder or a 30-clip admission.

The independently audited `3ec159d8` pilot supplies the pre-outcome cost evidence. Its 16/48-frame wall times were 32.83589077/68.29033494 seconds, giving fixed 15.10866868 seconds and marginal 1.10795138 seconds/frame. The prospective full-stage bound is `1.5 × (fixed + 188 × marginal) × max(1, current16 / prior16) + 40`, or 375.105 seconds at the measured 16-frame speed. The new 16-frame smoke must reproduce all five accepted pilot results, labels, metric-model identities and original calibration parity exactly before a full stage; timing must fit the 400-second full-stage cap and the remaining campaign. Bounds are 100 seconds smoke, 400 full, 550 fleet campaign and at most 600 GPU/800 CPU seconds inclusive. A malformed source, changed result, invalid GPU or timings, exhausted budget or timeout is an incomplete measurement and stops promotion.

The 48-frame pilot is already a negative limitation: disk and brightness matched original streak hit counts for both judges while deliberately wrong position scored 4/48 and 1/48. This experiment does not establish an appearance or timing advantage; that limitation remains beside any 188-frame result. The hypothesis is that extending the registered controls may characterize this limited sensitivity on the full already accepted clip; a competing explanation is that judges mainly respond to position and retain the same ambiguity for appearance/timing. Five-arm scores at one calibration point do not establish generality.
