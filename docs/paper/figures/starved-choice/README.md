[Player-body DISTS](players-rate-quality.pdf) and [ball-crop DISTS](ball-rate-quality.pdf) use one physical rally, 240 native delivered frames and identical source-reviewed supports at 12 ticks. Each player contributes equally. The ball crop covers 64 × 64 pixels around the reviewed center. [All operating points and source records](points.json) identify real measurements. Lines join points in a mode family that improve on every cheaper measured setting. These connections guide the eye and do not imply that an encoder achieves the intervening rates or that an online ABR decision has been validated.

The solid joint curve pays for both appearance images in its actual 4.8-second packet. Hollow points price identical rendered pixels in the scenario that the two previously loaded references remain valid, deducting their actual payload bytes. The two curves answer different delivery questions. A reference-only cross-shot test checks appearance and mapping at two observed shots. A full broadcast requires initialization, ordinary shots, reuse decisions, repairs and refreshes at their actual times. The separate multiple-match player results have a different 25/50-fps sampled-window protocol and two-hour cost model.

For the equal-quality comparison, use the repository's common-support PCHIP BD-rate calculation on the improving candidate settings and the upper concave hull of all compatible ordinary AV1, ROI and neural anchor encodes. Record both the full measured range and the 20–125 kbps decision region, using actual rates from 19 to 126 kbps for the latter to allow achieved-rate differences around the endpoints. Also preserve the available discrete winner at each budget. A candidate within 0.5% of a target qualifies for a matched-rate comparison, but the discrete budget selection cannot spend above its cap. Include numeric support and hull sensitivity; no metric extrapolation. A body BD-rate over a vanishing quality interval does not support a substantive player claim. The reference-free group remains a scenario even if it wins a selector.
