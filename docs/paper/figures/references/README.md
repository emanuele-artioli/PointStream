AVIF achieved the highest still-image PSNR for each player at every tested budget. The Q46 payloads are also the same size as the older Q45 encodes. These two images nominate shared rungs for a motion test. At run time the reference budget comes from the session rate, valid reference lifetime, refreshes and other stream bytes. A short or interrupted session gives a different available image budget. A reference already present at the receiver has a different switching cost from a new reference.

The blinded author review accepted Q65 for Player 2 and rejected it for Player 1. Rendering success alone does not qualify a rung. If the middle rung passes, check the lower rung and only test a higher rung if motion or a rate decision justifies it. Freeze the resulting ladder across unseen players. ### When to send a reference

Use the still-image comparison in the appendix. For the main experiment, choose among keeping a valid cached reference, sending one shared image codec and quality for both players, or using conventional coding. Define the remaining budget in **bytes** over a conservative predicted use interval: available link bytes minus the chosen base, poses, ball, headers, model initialization, expected updates and fallback reserve. Also require that any new reference arrive by its playback deadline. Estimate the use interval from information available at the sender when it chooses (observed time until previous changes, shot type, cache identity and buffer). Recompute on a cut, invalidation, bandwidth change or return. The measured future duration is reserved for retrospective scoring.

For each feasible choice use a development lookup of rendered player quality and the opportunity cost of spending those bytes on the rest of the video. Pick the lowest predicted total distortion among admissible choices. Keep the valid cache if a refresh adds too little measured quality to justify its bytes. If no qualified object-coded choice fits, use the conventional candidate. For example, at 20 kbps even **all** of a 9.6-second budget is only 24,000 bytes. The lowest tested AVIF pair costs 24,463 image bytes before transport or a single video frame. The same reference can be affordable on a longer visit, after prefetch that was charged earlier, or from a valid cache.

**Smallest policy test.** The two shared Q65 and Q78 reviews are complete. The last common-rung trial tested Q90 against the lossless control. Record per-player rendered distortion and temporal artifacts, complete startup bytes, time to first frame, actual cache hits and refreshes. For one short start and one observed return at the planned total-rate budgets, compare the resulting shared policy with always-refresh at a fixed quality, always-reuse while valid, and conventional coding. Include the actual bytes and quality of all fallback frames. Choose rungs and duration estimates on development footage, then freeze them for held-out players and matches. A crossover that persists on fresh passages earns a main-text panel of chosen rung, realized bitrate and displayed quality. Until then the policy is a design to test.

## Rendered appearance and author judgments

The [rendered-reference figure](rendered-reference-quality.pdf) plots the three shared AVIF image-payload budgets against 24-frame RGB PSNR on the complete player crop. Each plotted curve uses the original generated tensors and the same source-derived boxes and source pixels; the background within the crop contributes to the score. The dashed line gives the lossless reference's rendered quality as a horizontal comparison and does not place lossless at the AVIF byte axis. The author accepted Q65 and Q78 for Player 2, while both were almost acceptable for Player 1 and thus fell below the preregistered acceptance rule. Lossless passed both. This is one author's development judgment on two players, and the Q78 outputs were independently validated after their lifecycle timed out under contention. The subsequent shared Q90 image pair costs 107,276 raw image bytes and passes the 30% saving precondition against the 242,317-byte lossless pair. The full-crop measurement on its 24 generated frames is [available](../../../experiments/common-reference-q90-20261122/rendered-crop-metrics.json). Its 107,276 bytes would require 11.80 active seconds at 125 kbps, or 67.54 seconds at 65 kbps, on the same measured 52.293 kbps ongoing delivery. On the featured 4.8-second serve, even the Q90 images alone would cost 178.79 kbps if charged entirely to that rally, before the background, object data or framing. A cheaper reference can shorten the reuse period needed; it cannot fit this cold start into 20–33 kbps. The author accepted both randomized candidates for each player.
