# Three-match low-rate ball comparison

[Figure](ball-starved.pdf) · [numeric selection](summary.json) · [experiment and receipts](../../../experiments/ball-confirmation-starved-20261122/README.md)

Each panel shows the predeclared tracked-ball target point and the lowest DISTS among the available plain or fitted-ROI AV1 encodes that cost no more than it. The line connects two measured results; it is not a rate–distortion curve. The selected baselines are 320×180 ROI on 209, native 720p plain on 269, and 320×180 plain on 235. The tracked stream uses the 320×180 base, 32×32 native tiles and a real packet, with tile CRF20 for 25-fps clips and CRF50 for the 60-fps clip. All outputs preserve source fps and are scored at 1280×720 against the same official visible labels: 9, 12 and 11. Rates include packet bytes for tracked and IVF bytes for AV1; no padding or interpolation. The 209 and 235 pairs differ by 1.6% and 2.9%; 269 differs by 18.7%. Therefore this is an affordable-anchor comparison, not an equal-rate curve. Other tested 269 720p controls include 61.90 kbps / 0.2666 DISTS, above the tracked rate. The 235 native controls cost 105–131 kbps. One source favors AV1 even at the lower rate. These are three short, previously opened source windows, not a population estimate or a complete-codec result. No DCVC or whole-frame comparison is present in this figure.

The metric is DISTS on 64×64 ball crops around official source labels; lower is better. Tracking positions come from the previously fitted tracker, not the labels. The source and evaluation receipts record frames, model hashes, encoder/decoder, per-frame scores and actual data rates. The figure makes no detector-accuracy or user-study claim.
