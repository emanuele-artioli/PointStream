# Reconstructing court behind foreground

A background replacement must fill pixels hidden by a player at the moment the frame was captured. This pilot asks how well three simple reconstruction policies recover court pixels that are actually known. It creates artificial foreground-shaped occlusions on four fixed frames from the existing opened 240-frame Nadal–Kyrgios rally, where the underlying court is visible. It reports errors inside those synthetic occlusions against the true pixels. Actual player-hidden pixels have no direct ground truth in the same frame, and this comparison does not establish their reconstruction quality.

Freeze the source, sender removal masks, target ticks and geometry before scoring. Use targets 30, 90, 150 and 210, two fixed 64×104 rectangles in the court, and keep only pixels outside each target's sender foreground mask. Use up to 16 evenly spaced donor frames, exclude the target, compute target-specific ORB homographies outside the archived foreground masks and the synthetic target mask, and require at least 50 inliers. Warp donor frames and their foreground masks into the target, then take the per-pixel temporal median only where a donor sees that point. To check missing-coverage handling, withhold all donor observations for the right half of each test mask in a second *simulated* condition. This creates two availability conditions on the same hidden pixels; it does not claim the rally naturally lacked those observations.

Compare spatial Telea inpainting of the target mask with the existing temporal-median-plus-Telea hole fill policy. The pure temporal estimate is scored only on pixels with valid donor coverage, alongside spatial and hybrid on that same subset. On all masked pixels, compare only spatial and hybrid. Report pooled RGB MSE and PSNR, temporal coverage, per-frame values and timing; do not infer transmitted bytes or equivalent-rate codec quality. Score the known target images only after the output is fixed. Additional quality and bitstream tests on naturally obscured court, camera changes, scene return, moving graphics and objects remain necessary.

Use a CPU-only smoke on the first target to confirm that both aligned donor and intentionally withheld supports are nonempty, residual pixels are finite, and no target pixels are used in the donor median. Forecast a 4-target run under 300 CPU-seconds and 360 elapsed seconds. The established production code uses ORB, homography, a masked temporal median and Telea to fill missing reference-plate pixels. This targeted experiment uses the same primitive policies with target-specific alignment to expose their strengths and limits. The experiment excludes any target image pixels in the artificial mask from alignment. The source masks are automatic and can miss foreground. The sender and decoder costs, including the bits needed to convey the reconstructed background and motion, are not measured.

## Development results

The 16-donor available condition covered 99.85% of the synthetic test pixels across the four targets. Spatial inpainting had pooled RGB MSE **171.34**, compared with **5.14** for aligned donors with hole filling. The temporal median alone had MSE **4.38** on pixels visible to at least one donor. In the deliberately half-withheld condition, measured donor coverage was 49.11%, and the aligned-donors-plus-hole-fill MSE rose to **62.34** across the complete test region. In this condition the spatial-only baseline stayed at **171.34**. These are source-pixel diagnostics on one known background, with a fixed mask and four paired samples. The input broadcast contains camera motion, changing illumination and segmentation errors, and the target mask construction excludes only the archived automatic foreground estimate.

The [draft quantitative figure](court-reconstruction.pdf) shows the all-pixel baseline once and the two donor-coverage conditions. The [numeric receipt](result.json) also retains the donor-visible subset, each frame's error sums, pixel counts, donor homographies, inlier counts, input digests, runtime and local environment. Four donor groups took 30.04 aggregate CPU seconds and 29.61 wall seconds with no GPU. The target-frame feature matcher excludes both the actual and synthetic foreground masks. The private native-image examples for target 90 are in `/private/tmp/ps-background-figure-verified-20261122/`; publish only after source rights are checked.

## Paper decision and next comparison

Present temporal reuse as a background-construction component in the system diagram. A main-text example should show a real foreground mask, aligned donor observations, the resulting clean background and the final composite, alongside their total bytes and an equivalent-rate baseline. The present artificial-mask figure is a development diagnostic for an appendix: its donors almost completely cover this one court region, and its missing-data case was deliberately imposed. It does not establish that donors will recover every shot or beat a modern video-inpainting model. The existing private strip shows an actual player outside the two synthetic test masks, so it must be labeled carefully and must not be mistaken for the result of removing that player.

Inpainting remains necessary for regions unseen in eligible donor frames, unreliable alignments, disocclusions, changing court surfaces and dynamic scenery. Registration errors can make observed donor pixels unsuitable as well. Existing video-inpainting research combines temporal propagation and learned spatial/temporal completion, for example [ProPainter](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/papers/Zhou_ProPainter_Improving_Propagation_and_Transformer_for_Video_Inpainting_ICCV_2023_paper.pdf). The temporal reuse itself is therefore an established ingredient; any paper contribution needs to come from measured integration into a transmitted, decoded scene and its rate–quality tradeoff.

For a targeted follow-up, freeze player-and-equipment masks and matched footage across several rallies including a court return and moving-camera shot. First compare donor coverage and actual composite output against source-visible held-out pixels, retaining the hard cases with poor alignment or absent observations. Compare the existing aligned-donor plus Telea fill against Telea alone and one practical video-inpainting model with fixed inputs and settings. Measure flicker, artifacts around real removals, and initialization, update and fallback bytes with the same encoder settings and then at matched output quality. Include spatial/temporal cost and disclose sender look-ahead. Add stronger models only if the first comparison would change the chosen background path or its paper claim. The present source-image diagnostic cannot establish that equal-rate result.
