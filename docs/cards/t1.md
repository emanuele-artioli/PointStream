## Background (for the item file and the user)
PointStream splits a video into foreground F (what the viewer watches) and
background, and codes each with its own tool. On egocentric video F is a
median 24% of the frame; coding F costs 36–116 kbps, and once it is billed
the split loses on every metric against every anchor (H5p 4a; the key
carrier of 4c, a flat colour outside F inside the foreground stream, also
fails there). Where F was 2% of the frame (item P09_106) the key carrier
was nearly free and the split won (−30% weighted DISTS). On broadcast
tennis the players and ball are a few percent of the frame. Every
keypoint renderer for hands and players has failed its bar, so the open
question for tennis is the pixel split itself, with F paid for.

## Goal
Decide whether the split on tennis (players and ball as coded pixels in the
foreground stream, the ball mode of K5 where it pays, the background in its

shelf, as an achievable envelope; neither side venue-adapted, unless both
are) on K6's 8 windows once F is billed, by BD-rate per metric (AGENTS.md,
Evidence).
