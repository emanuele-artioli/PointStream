# Primary predecessor lineage and paper reuse

This card reviews local primary manuscript text, not published experimental reproduction. The sources are PRESLEY revision `07b46952dc2783ef8857b277795fed58ff777a37`, `sections/presley.tex`, and GenStream revision `05ffd90a711a537b5053c5f76eaf64a36e7cb89f`, `GenStream/main.tex`. The current thesis checkout is `903f73a600807ee0f26bfd09d6f5540331c688a0` and no longer has that GenStream path; read-only `git show` recovered the historical primary text. Publication status and underlying predecessor experiments were not recertified.

**Research question.** What continuous idea survives from selective degradation through semantic streaming, and which receiver assumptions change?

**Observation.** PRESLEY preserves the frame geometry, replaces or degrades selected blocks in place, and restores at the client from the decoded picture plus a level map. Its primary text says that the map is bit-plane packed and DEFLATE-compressed and included in `transmitted_size_bytes`. It explicitly distinguishes hole filling from conditioned downsampling restoration. This is more specific than generic ROI coding, and provides a useful cost-of-restoration lineage. Its own hard foreground exclusion makes the quoted background restoration comparisons narrower than PointStream player synthesis.

GenStream sends keypoints, boxes and camera viewpoints and composites a generated skater into a pre-scanned venue. Its primary text reports 64 kB for 17-joint motion, under 4 kB boxes, 42 kB viewpoints, approximately 110 kB total against 134 MB HEVC. Its reported >99.9% reduction applies to continuous metadata and expressly excludes one-time venue/model distribution. The abstract describes partial validation. The body also contains broad fidelity, live-streaming and stall-free language which that arithmetic alone does not establish. Keep the exciting representation question but do not inherit those claims as PointStream evidence.

**Limitations and later corrections.** Neither paper is a PointStream ablation or matched modern tennis benchmark. The numerical GenStream ratio is a reported predecessor observation conditional on a prepaid client; it is not evidence that an event-specific tennis payload is cheaper, faithful, causal or runnable on a new client. Modern conventional reference reuse further prevents treating background memory as unique semantic-codec novelty. The pinned prior package `b427edff5146df0b14060c05681e7bcb7e1237fb` states these boundaries in Chapters 1–4 and 6; the recovered code cards independently constrain PointStream JE10/11.

**Proposed paper-use paragraph.** “Selective degradation methods make restoration a rate-allocation problem: the savings in the transmitted picture must pay for reconstruction information. Semantic streaming takes that question further by transmitting motion and scene descriptions. For broadcast tennis, PointStream tests whether object appearance, geometry and a background representation can preserve the delivered action within a complete transport and client-computation budget. Scene assets, sequence-specific adaptation and corrective data are therefore explicit costs rather than assumed prerequisites.”

**Disposition.** Both local primary manuscripts analyzed for lineage and accounting assumptions. Their experimental artifacts and final publication versions remain outside this bounded recovery wave. No broad literature search was performed.

# Two evidence-conditioned paper directions

**Direction A: measured object-centric codec.** Retain the background/object/residual architecture and explicit lattice as a method. A successful paper would require a fresh payload-only decoder, qualified metrics, complete charged rate ladders and named conventional plus runnable generative competitors on frozen tennis sources. Show component value and event geometry under equal-rate controls, uncertainty and a declared client budget. Only then can the prose state the measured frontier improvement. Conventional headroom and setup-versus-recurring hypotheses motivate this direction; they do not achieve it.

**Proposed paragraph conditional on future evidence.** “We evaluate the object representation by comparing fully delivered reconstructions and complete streams on identical source windows. Each component is enabled and disabled under the same reference policy, allowing its quality benefit to be weighed against its transmitted cost. Results are reported separately for pixel fidelity, perceptual reconstruction and independently annotated action geometry, together with decoder resources.” This is a protocol paragraph now, and becomes a results paragraph only with qualified artifacts.

**Direction B: rigorous boundary/negative-result study.** If fresh-client full-wire comparisons remain negative or unresolved, center the paper on when sparse object representations fail to turn altered-target headroom into actual transport savings. Preserve repaired metrics, background registration/refresh cost, silhouette/appearance transport, small-player conditioning and source-access audits; compare simple coded/warped controls and qualified generative methods under a common protocol. A collection of implementation failures alone is insufficient: distinguish incomplete instruments from valid negative operating points, and establish reproducible mechanisms and scope.

**Proposed paragraph conditional on supported negative evidence.** “Removing foreground content reduces conventional coding cost in the tested altered-target diagnostic, but reconstructing the original action imposes additional appearance, geometry and correction costs. We study this gap with complete byte ledgers and isolated decoder inputs. The tested configurations reveal where registration and foreground restoration consume the apparent opportunity, without treating one checkpoint failure as a general limit of generative video coding.” Numerical clauses require the original saved observations and their comparability boundaries.
