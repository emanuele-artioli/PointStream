## Card A — the early rejected draft contains claims the surviving evidence cannot support

**Primary source:** `2c967ea324da855e4231ce6ca65680668ff4a758`, `main.tex` (Abstract, Introduction, Problem Description and Motivation). Reviewer context: `94fcc1b73666150c87fdfc7fdec54a1fe6031eaf`, `reviews.md`.

The May 2026 manuscript described PointStream as a *live* hybrid codec and claimed the semantic path would never be worse than an AV1 fallback. Its abstract claimed adequate perceptual quality at about 60 VMAF below 200 kbps, with state-of-the-art live codecs needing 7–10× more bandwidth. The motivation reported about 60% less data after separating objects and background, then another 36% reduction to 2.92 MB after pose-conditioned cGAN reconstruction. It explicitly omitted VMAF for this latter step and assumed that the generated players would have no noticeable artifacts. The prose itself calls the example non-rigorous, but that caveat does not repair the abstract's quantitative system claim. A size sentence also gives `230 KB` and `294 MB` where the surrounding comparison says 950 KB and 1.2 MB; the units are internally implausible and cannot be silently corrected from this source.

The ACM MM reviews stored in `reviews.md` are useful context for the TOMM revisions, not experimental evidence. They identify narrow tennis-only scope, missing AV1/VVC and learned/generative codec comparisons, no visual or temporal-coherence validation, no encode/decode speed comparison, and weak support for the quality claim. The stored ratings range from clear/rejection to marginal acceptance, with one positive review. They explain the revision agenda but do not establish whether any method works.

The manuscript's own later audit withdrew the inherited “60 VMAF at <200 Kbps, 7–10×” claim on 20 August because no evidence then supported it and a full-match run selected the conventional fallback on every observed sub-chunk. By 29 August, a paired ladder directly reported a rate loss in the measured configuration. The safe paper history is therefore “an early draft claimed this; subsequent evidence and audits withdrew it,” never a softened version of the old headline.

**Limitations / later corrections:** The May text is a proposed method narrative, not a valid rate–distortion experiment. It uses one 8-second 4K/50 fps test description, omits calibrated quality for its generative step, assumes preservation of appearance and background quality, and does not show complete transmitted payload accounting. The reported MB unit conflict is unresolved. The over-the-air live and fallback guarantees were later removed or explicitly treated as unproven.

**Proposed paper use:** If history is relevant, state that early pilot figures were withdrawn after instrument, accounting, and full-pipeline audits; do not reproduce the figures as preliminary evidence. A current abstract should make only a claim supported by the frozen comparison protocol and current artifacts.

## Card B — evaluation-path defects void early generator rankings

**Primary manuscript/log sources:** `48b75ed20968f6963b7ef7748e15d7a6a82fbfb8:RESEARCH_LOG.md`; `0603f65d7724ed65499ec69281731052831e4045:RESEARCH_LOG.md` and `main.tex`; later manuscript record `6f86e3b27b73b82b8490b8b5ab17f4acb2d658c3:sections/evaluation.tex`.

The July G2 successive-halving campaign reported 12-clip generator figures, including Pix2Pix PSNR 22.98 / VMAF 15.79, SPADE PSNR 21.89 / VMAF 3.00, and pose-ControlNet PSNR 9.76 / VMAF 0.11. The log explicitly retracts the whole ranking: the campaign never completed a rung (state remained rung 0; one termination was a `TypeError`, another a CUDA OOM and later interruption), log rows were out of order, and the score path had a condition/decoder mismatch. The 0.11 VMAF reading was called a defect signature, not weak-model evidence. Additional problems found in the phase-0 audit included 33% wrong pairings and 23% missing pairs for the relevant ControlNet training data.

A fixed-path remeasurement on the same 12 probe clips changed the values materially: ControlNet pose moved from 9.76 to 16.06 dB PSNR (+6.3 dB); Pix2Pix fell from 22.98 to 15.39 dB. The log says the old ControlNet ranking was “far too low” and old Pix2Pix score “far too high.” Crop VMAF was near zero for both and saturated on 512×512 padded actor crops, so the revised record says it cannot rank these crops. The fixed run still had poor absolute scores, and the training pairs remained defective, so the correction does not turn it into a successful generator result.

A second early metric problem affected LPIPS. The former backend computed an uncalibrated VGG feature distance under the name LPIPS; unrelated and good reconstructions scored about 0.083 and 0.085, showing almost no useful range. A published LPIPS package with AlexNet features replaced it. Separately, the FFmpeg `libvmaf` inputs were reversed, allowing a blurred clip to score 100 while an identical clip scored 97.4. The manuscript then invalidated *all pre-23-August perceptual numbers*.

A further delivery-path defect was documented with the 29-August paired ladder: before 28 August, the `coded_roundtrip` decode step did not name an output codec, so FFmpeg re-encoded the reconstruction as Matroska/libx264 at its default CRF. This changed the delivered reconstruction itself; it was not merely a bad scorer. The manuscript flags all pre-28-August qualities from that path as suspect. This is a separate defect from the earlier reversed metric inputs.

**Limitations / later corrections:** These dated log numbers are repair history, not current citable measurements. Fixing the scorer/path does not repair bad training pair identity, missing examples, checkpoint provenance, crop-scale metric suitability, or appearance conditioning. Keep the fixed-path 16.06/15.39 values as a diagnostic comparison only, not a result that establishes a winner.

**Proposed paper use:** The method section may say that early generator rankings were excluded after detecting paired-data and evaluation-path errors. A current quantitative generator claim needs a calibrated metric at the evaluated resolution, exact clip/checkpoint identity, a verified target/reference pairing, native invocation mode, and a consistent static-copy baseline.

## Card C — the lattice is a design and evaluation contract, not a demonstrated ablation result

**Primary sources:** `933044486780f38871f304164103f61066742acf:main.tex` (concepts moved from blueprint into manuscript); `7aa43186462902d16b74b8bccf46f35a915546f1:main.tex`, `sections/evaluation.tex`, `sections/system_design.tex`; construction/test claim updates in `dfe7b49568ca9e1af3d533b533c9889d21681b20:sections/system_design.tex` and the paired merge `3424ff1386207ab0140f87fdc6e7bf3861e2cf76`.

The August design decomposes each object stream into independent coordinates for appearance (compressed crop, diffusion latent, or image embedding), motion (keypoints, sparse trajectories, or encoded crop video), and temporal policy (metadata sparsity, generation sparsity, and pipeline sparsity). It also exposes background model and residual choices. A valid lattice point must have a registered decoder that consumes its chosen appearance and motion representation. The paper argues that rate is the shared currency and that each component earns inclusion only by improving a swept rate–distortion curve against the all-off, conventional-video corner. A one-point byte delta or isolated quality gain is generally not sufficient.

The paper records construction-level evidence: all declared lattice corners build, the all-off corner is the source path, a disabled detector adds zero cost, and residual-absent versus residual-lossless changes the payload. These are implementation/contracts evidence. The same design-status comment says no end-to-end lattice sweep exists and that no quantitative component ablation is landed. Racket heuristics, background layering, temporal policy, richer keypoints, and semantic routing are still questions rather than demonstrated codec benefits. The broad “never worse” outcome-safe routing guarantee was also withdrawn: the one July full-match run chose fallback for every observed sub-chunk, so it established no semantic-path win.

The ROI appendix reinforces the need for actual encoder checks. `4e164a71f59759ac754857257c19688f6a40f86c:appendices/roi_verification.tex` records that AVC `addroi` under fixed QP produced byte-identical 7,627-byte streams and a 0.00 dB region delta in the tested 640×384/20-frame probe; the filter was only active under CRF, which is outside the comparison contract. The AV1 ROI probe at matched QP spent 3.08% more bytes for +0.35 dB inside and 0.00 dB outside, so it did not answer whether ROI improves quality at matched rate. HEVC's ROI surface remained unverified; VVC had no caller-controlled region map in the surveyed build. No fair region-controlled ladder was run by 29 August.

**Limitations / later corrections:** A buildable/configurable lattice does not show that its coordinates save bits, preserve quality, or compose into a useful codec. The actual decoder roster couples some coordinates. Claims about quality-safe residuals, per-component value, and fallback guarantees remain open. ROI flags, parser acceptance, or a matched-QP quality movement are not enough to claim a fair rate–distortion baseline.

**Proposed paper use:** Describe the lattice as the system's explicit configuration/evaluation space. Report its construction tests separately from codec evidence. State plainly that the ablation curves and matched-rate ROI arms remain unrun; do not present the system architecture or unit tests as results that components pay for themselves.

## Card D — headroom evolves from a synthetic probe to a scoped 8-scene diagnostic

**Primary sources:** synthetic and first real-4K transitions in `4c8f65f0ebaddc1fafaee378b84e5047c442cd95:sections/problem.tex`; detailed two-scene correction in `8b1b27f2ad47ff97741814f728417d17cdd287d9:sections/problem.tex`; final eight-scene table/protocol in `f32c1cf9cb70e74b2dfcb86f3244408637d46e21:sections/problem.tex` and `appendices/headroom_measurement.tex`; later compact table revisions in `e7164916ed7faa833330116eb9dd52d5d771c19a`, `d4520a5ddc7513624d75d009b610bdf53a00e301`, and `6b5b7fabf9d281091fc11b7ee7c10de85207d537`.

The first provisional probe was synthetic 96×128 tennis, 16 frames, three seeds, and libx264 QP 32/40/48. It reported 12.2% foreground-removal BD-rate saving (SE 0.26 pp) and a 17.4× background ratio against JPEG-coding a filled background. The revision itself notes synthetic players covered about 4.7% of pixels versus roughly 2.1% in the real broadcast footage, and warns that still-image JPEG overstated the conventional background cost. This probe was replaced, not promoted as final evidence.

The first real-footage revision used two 48-frame 4K broadcast tennis scenes. It reported foreground savings 15.2–26.0% with players occupying 0.55–1.02% of pixels, and background savings 34.4–68.9% against conventional inter-coding. It warned that the sample was only n=2, that VVC used a different QP due to an empty output at the chosen QP, and that these changed-target conventional-coding savings were not PointStream performance. A later eight-scene/four-codec/four-to-six-match version narrowed the foreground saving headline to 14.2–18.3% per-codec means and the background headline to 64.3–78.0% means. It reports player area 1.11±0.32% and an AVC bitrate concentration of 18.9±5.0× (per-clip savings divided by player area, not ratio-of-means). The eight windows are 48 frames at 3840×2160 and 24 fps from six matches.

All four foreground columns have eight reported per-scene values. The background appendix omits values for inadequate curve overlap (for example, AVC and HEVC on two rows). The expanded appendix reports foreground means of AVC 17.0±3.1%, HEVC 18.3±3.4%, AV1 15.4±2.8%, and VVC 14.2±2.6%. The 26 August compact summary records eligible background counts of AVC n=6, HEVC n=7, AV1 n=8, and VVC n=8, with means 64.3±8.4%, 66.5±7.3%, 78.0±5.6%, and 76.1±3.9%, respectively. The final per-scene table marks excluded overlaps; do not describe every background mean as based on n=8.

The late-August correction expressly retracts a simple VVC-generation gap. The early two-scene report's roughly 7.7 percentage-point VVC shortfall was confounded by QP 47 rather than 48 and a shifted integration interval. The expanded analysis reports paired AVC–VVC differences of +0.028±0.015 at common QP and +0.023±0.017 on a common PSNR slice, and avoids a codec-generation trend claim. Some scenes have almost no foreground headroom: `djokovic_zverev/scene_002` is 1.1% AVC saving and `federer_djokovic/scene_003` 9.9%. The narrative must retain this spread; a mean does not imply every clip benefits.

**Interpretation boundary:** This removes/changes the target by inpainting players and compares conventional codec curves against each arm's own target. It diagnoses where conventional codecs spend bits; it is not an end-to-end PointStream encode, does not attribute measured savings to the PointStream representation, and does not prove acceptable generated-player quality. The paper records pre-registered bounds and exact encoder versions, but raw artifacts were not independently revalidated in this extraction.

**Proposed paper use:** “Across the eligible 4K tennis windows, inpainting player regions changed the conventional-codec BD-rate by a mean 14.2–18.3% across the tested codecs, while player silhouettes averaged 1.11±0.32% of frame area. This changed-target headroom diagnostic motivates object-centric coding; it does not measure PointStream's total rate or reconstruction quality.” Add exact valid n, overlap exclusions, and the two low-saving clips wherever those figures are reported.

## Card E — calibrated metrics narrow what quality claims mean

**Primary sources:** calibration correction in `5c3a5b3a21a62d9d0540c80cf833d7d33644e8de:sections/evaluation.tex`; resolution-specific clarification in `6cfcf3dec232280fecdfa0275446605b2dab5f12:sections/evaluation.tex` (same patch is duplicated at `6060444ac23fef084286e7c8c5ae27453b21f00f`); final calibration table in `e7164916ed7faa833330116eb9dd52d5d771c19a:appendices/evaluation_notes.tex` and `6f86e3b27b73b82b8490b8b5ab17f4acb2d658c3:sections/evaluation.tex`.

The final manuscript calibrates on two native 4K broadcast frames. At 4K, LPIPS (AlexNet) was 0.0000 for an identical pair, 0.0171 for mild blur, 0.2982 for severe blur, and 0.5493 for an unrelated match clip. At 960×540 the last two reverse order: severe blur 0.6130, unrelated content 0.5220. A fixed-size blur kernel affects a larger proportion of the lower-resolution frame; calibration ranges therefore do not transfer across resolution. VMAF is 97.54 for identical pairs on this material, not 100, and floors at 0 for severe blur and unrelated content. The manuscript says VMAF is a frame-level metric here: libvmaf refuses sides under 32 pixels and is not useful on a flat player crop; region-scoped LPIPS is the designated crop metric.

These anchor values are calibration observations on specified content and resolution, not universal metric ceilings. The paper also calls for reporting object, background, and whole-frame scores at their respective scopes, since a small player region can be badly reconstructed while the whole-frame score remains high. A 12-clip object PSNR standard deviation of about 2 dB implies an SE near 0.6 dB, per its note; a roughly 1 dB effect is only about 1.7 standard errors and should not name a winner.

**Limitations / later corrections:** Any pre-23-August LPIPS/VMAF ranking is explicitly void. The manuscript's 29-August note separately flags quality from the old coded-roundtrip path through 27 August as contaminated by an implicit lossy re-encode. Metric correctness and decode-path correctness are distinct gates. Do not use the 97.54 ceiling or the LPIPS ordering as evidence that a generator is perceptually good; they only establish response on this small calibration set.

**Proposed paper use:** Identify each metric's region, resolution, and role. Use calibrated 4K LPIPS for object similarity and frame-level VMAF only for whole-frame content under the tested setup. Say that LPIPS ordering changed after downscaling; do not compare 4K and 540p scores as if the scale were invariant.

## Card F — generator prose and table contradict one another on PSNR and on one LPIPS comparator

**Primary sources:** `6f86e3b27b73b82b8490b8b5ab17f4acb2d658c3:sections/evaluation.tex`; appendix matrix `e7164916ed7faa833330116eb9dd52d5d771c19a:appendices/evaluation_notes.tex` (same table remains in the final commit).

The final evaluation prose and status comments say the static-copy floor “beats every generator on object PSNR and LPIPS.” The appendix table does not support the PSNR half: the static keyframe copy is 11.82 dB, Pose-ControlNet 12.03 dB, Segmentation-ControlNet 12.19 dB, and the appendix table gives Animate-Anyone native clip as 11.57 dB. The evaluation STATUS summary elsewhere gives Animate-Anyone 12.21 dB / 0.5692 LPIPS, so even the AA PSNR roster disagrees across sections. Higher PSNR is better; no PSNR standard errors or paired uncertainty are supplied. For LPIPS, lower is better and the table does show the static-copy row at 0.451±0.022 below Pose-ControlNet 0.603±0.024, Segmentation-ControlNet 0.560±0.021, and Animate-Anyone native clip 0.569±0.021. That can support a scoped LPIPS-only probe observation, subject to the protocol caveats below, but not “wins on both metrics.”

There is a second unresolved comparison mismatch in the evaluation's NOTE: Animate-Anyone clip mode is said to score 0.570 LPIPS versus a “static copy at the same offset” of 0.582, which would favor AA slightly on LPIPS in that paired condition. The appendix instead compares it with the 0.451±0.022 keyframe static-copy floor. The manuscript does not establish whether those are different offset/paste protocols or a stale comparison. Keep both source statements visible in the audit; do not select the favorable one without resolving exact source identities and masks.

The table describes 12 probe clips but gives no PSNR dispersion. Animate-Anyone fine-tuning has seen all seven tennis source videos, including videos later described as held out, so its reported result is in-domain only. The generator comparison is development triage; it does not qualify an unseen-match generalization claim. The manuscript notes that ControlNet has no dedicated appearance pathway and that its text caption channel was inert, which narrows the negative to these tested engines/conditioning paths. The IP-Adapter result was still awaiting a corrected score and was marked not citable.

**Proposed paper use:** A conservative statement is: “In the 12-clip probe matrix, the tested pose/segmentation ControlNet and native-clip Animate-Anyone runs had higher (worse) region LPIPS than the static keyframe-copy row. Their PSNR values do not show a uniform loss: Pose- and Segmentation-ControlNet exceed the copy row, and uncertainty is not reported. Animate-Anyone was trained on all seven tennis videos, so this is not held-out evidence.” Before using even that sentence in a paper, resolve the 0.582 same-offset copy versus 0.451 keyframe copy discrepancy and recheck artifacts/checkpoints.

## Card G — the paired rate ladder and residual sweep are scoped negatives, not a PointStream win

**Primary source:** `6f86e3b27b73b82b8490b8b5ab17f4acb2d658c3:sections/evaluation.tex`; antecedent accounting clarification `d7bd14ffdb2f11fca063a03f88b41c340eb8a694:sections/evaluation.tex`.

On eight 4K frames from two tennis windows, the paired ladder compares PointStream and each conventional codec at one fixed preset per codec, across overlapping luma-PSNR curves. For the most static window, it reports PointStream rate penalties of +116.8% versus AV1 across 39.45–44.02 dB overlap, +166.8% versus HEVC, +165.9% versus AVC, and +378.1% versus VVC. Those four penalty magnitudes must not be ranked against each other: encoder preset effort is not matched across codecs. On the most dynamic window, PointStream tops out at 31.0 dB while AV1's lowest-rate rung is about 38.03 dB; there is no overlap and no BD-rate is defined. The paper says the static clip is the most favorable available.

This measured configuration disables generation: it sends a plate, pasted crops, and residual. The plate is JPEG-coded and contributes 88–91% of payload at each rung, while residual contributes 3–9%. Even without residual, plate plus crops costs 487,643 B at 35.37 dB versus AV1's 85,995 B at 39.45 dB. The residual itself is effective at its local task: on the static window the absent/coarse/medium/fine rungs are 487,643 B/35.37 dB, 491,977 B/40.77 dB, 508,413 B/43.33 dB, and 812,971 B/46.11 dB. Thus the coarse residual adds 0.9% payload for +5.40 dB; medium adds 4.3% for +7.96 dB. On the dynamic window, residual quality gain reaches 14.83 dB over an unaided 18.36 dB reconstruction. Lossless residual was 398,618,843 B and excluded as a dense calibration array, not a real rate point.

The plate code itself remains an unoptimized axis. A one-plate matched-fidelity probe at around 38 dB found JPEG 283,431 B, AV1 intra 79,726 B, and VVC intra 68,477 B (about 3.6–4.1× smaller than JPEG), but the still-image codec was not swept in the paired codec evaluation. A separate note records between-point panorama inter-frame coding 31–53% cheaper in AV1, versus 1.2–3.3% for consecutive same-scene frames; the design is marked measured-but-unimplemented, codec-dependent, and not run under the low-delay/no-lookahead conditions required for the intended independence argument. These are candidate engineering directions, not corrected PointStream results.

**Limitations / later corrections:** Only 8 frames, two windows, luma PSNR, no region-controlled anchor ladder, no encode-time axis, and no generator. BD-rate comparison is valid only over stated overlaps. A local residual curve is not a whole-system advantage, especially when the JPEG plate dominates. The literature-like difference between AV1 and other encoder percentages is a preset comparison, not a cross-codec quality order. No system win was established by these commits.

**Proposed paper use:** “In the tested eight-frame configuration without generation, PointStream had higher rate than each paired conventional anchor on the static window and no overlapping PSNR range with AV1 on the dynamic window. The residual improved reconstruction efficiently, but it formed only 3–9% of payload; the JPEG plate formed 88–91%. These observations localize the measured deficit to plate coding in this implementation and do not establish the performance of a generative or panorama-delta system.” Add the specific overlap and penalty values only with the codec preset and frame/window identities.

## Card H — section-status contradictions remain in the final August snapshot

## Cross-card paper-use rules

1. Do not restore the May abstract claim, “never worse,” or any pre-audit generator ranking.
2. Separate changed-target conventional headroom from end-to-end PointStream rate–distortion measurements.
3. Preserve each observation's source window, resolution, frame count, target/region, metric calibration, encoder, selected preset/QP, and eligible overlap. Unknown or uninspected fields stay unknown.
4. State when generation was disabled, ROI arms were absent, or codec/time dimensions were not measured.
5. Keep contradictory manuscript values and later corrections side by side until raw records resolve them; do not silently pick a favorable number.
