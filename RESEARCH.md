# Research brief

Can one transmitted representation preserve the action for viewers at constrained rates and expose its motion to a machine? The first question is perceptual and the second asks what an application can read, how accurately, and at what processing cost. A conventional stream, ROI coding, tracked pixels, rendered trajectories and pose-conditioned synthesis are all credible choices. The [paper figure plan](PLAN.md) defines how to choose between them.

## Evidence for the main figures

**Viewer fidelity.** On one 4.8-second, 50-fps serve, the same 21.545 kbps PointStream packet reconstructed both players and the ball. Mean body DISTS was 0.262, versus 0.512 for ordinary AV1 at 21.623 kbps and 0.392 for DCVC-UF at 20.450 kbps. Ball-crop DISTS was 0.345, versus 0.413 and 0.344 respectively. Lower is better. All comparisons use the same 12 human-reviewed instants. With a strict 20 kbps cap, ball-only PointStream scored 0.3643 on the ball crop at 19.965 kbps, ordinary AV1 0.4181 at 19.892 kbps, and DCVC-UF 0.3459 at 19.135 kbps. The joint price assumes both appearances are cached. Charging initialization on this rally raises it to 428.777 kbps. Whole-frame RGB PSNR was 20.35, 23.20 and 27.58 dB respectively. Generated bodies also had greater motion-compensated error than ordinary AV1. The serve clip and separate player and ball curves show what each number measures. [Joint receiver and controls](docs/experiments/joint-trajectory-20261122/README.md)

**Across matches.** On eight historical windows from three physical matches, pose-conditioned players improved equal-match mean player-region DISTS by 0.0689 over DCVC-UF at the evaluated window-specific rates under two-hour appearance and adaptation reuse. The historical region includes the union of matte and pose support. In a separate ball study at a 60.6 kbps budget, sending tracked pixels beat the stronger conventional controls in two of three matches. Conventional video also led on ball appearance on the frozen Basel holdout. Use the full operating curves to identify where each mode helps. [Player windows](docs/experiments/player-rd-historical-20261122/README.md) · [Ball controls](docs/experiments/ball-640-controls-20261122/README.md) · [Basel](docs/experiments/ball-basel-confirmation-20261122/README.md)

**Machine access.** At 63.06 versus 63.36 kbps, the same detector located 5/261 visible balls on AV1 and 235/261 on the fitted-trajectory reconstruction. Detections on absent frames rose from 3/30 to 11/30. Reading the paid positions directly gave 240/261 and 2/30. On the prospectively selected Adelaide match, sending 130 extra bytes preserved all 64 ordinary video frames exactly and gave five direct hits on five visible labels versus zero for the detector. One location was emitted on seven not-visible labels. This supports a direct ball-location interface. The 24 reviewed player masks give a mixed result for direct silhouettes. [Dense test](docs/experiments/ball-matched-low-20261122/README.md) · [Adelaide](docs/experiments/ball-adelaide-prospective-20261122/README.md) · [Masks](docs/paper/figures/player-tasks/README.md)

**A receiver over time.** The actual indexed two-rally passage costs 30.798 kbps across 226.1 seconds, versus 30.414 kbps for conventional endpoints with the same intervening video. The original appearance pair cost 242,646 bytes; its return mapping cost 354. Even perfect object-coded endpoints could yield at most 24.53 dB whole-passage luma with that fallback, while continuous AV1 achieved 26.13 dB at 27.07 kbps. The main system figure should show both routes. A native AV1 control already achieves a 4.84% equal-quality rate saving by retaining a reference across a cutaway. [Passage bill](docs/paper/figures/system/passage-bill.pdf) · [Native reference experiment](docs/experiments/native-return-control-20261122/README.md)

## Submission decision

Write around scene-level choice, measured foreground fidelity and direct machine access. The leading joint result is one cache-qualified serve. An independent full-rate two-player and ball replay on a second physical match would strengthen the headline. Keep equipment outside the measured body region; the generated near player visibly loses racket grip and the tracked-pixel ball method gave no racket-quality gain across the three tested broadcasts.
