# Research brief

PointStream asks when sending the motion of an object helps more than spending the same bits on its image. Its receiver can use one delivery to reconstruct the scene for a viewer and expose object data to an application. Keep the architecture general. Use tennis to establish where each representation earns its bandwidth. [Paper figure plan](PLAN.md)

## Evidence for the main figures

**Player fidelity across matches.** At 33 kbps, equal-match player-region DISTS was **0.248 for PointStream, 0.402 for ordinary AV1 and 0.318 for DCVC-UF**. Seven windows from three matches support all three curves. Each method uses the same 80 sampled ticks per window. Rates interpolate within measured support and charge the historical appearance bank and same-match adaptation over two hours. PointStream and ordinary AV1 also cover an eighth window, averaging 0.249 and 0.401. [Pooled curves](docs/paper/figures/player-rd-historical/pooled-curve.pdf) · [Reference lifetime sensitivity](docs/paper/figures/player-rd-historical/reuse-33.pdf)

**Both objects in one stream.** On a 4.8-second serve, the joint packet used 21.545 kbps with cached appearances. Body DISTS was 0.262 and ball DISTS 0.345. Ordinary AV1 scored 0.512 and 0.413 at 21.623 kbps. DCVC-UF scored 0.392 and 0.344 at 20.450 kbps. Both bodies improved on every reviewed instant against both codecs. Whole-frame RGB PSNR and body motion error favored DCVC-UF. Charging initialization raises PointStream to 428.777 kbps with the common weights installed. [Full receiver and four-way serve](docs/experiments/joint-trajectory-20261122/README.md)

**Choosing a ball representation.** Under a strict 20 kbps ceiling, DCVC-UF, ball-only PointStream and ordinary AV1 scored 0.346, 0.364 and 0.418 ball DISTS. At a 60.6 kbps budget, tracked pixels led on two of three matches. Ordinary video led on the Basel holdout. These findings motivate choosing a mode by both rate and task. [Ball curves](docs/paper/figures/starved-choice/ball-rate-quality.pdf) · [Three matches](docs/experiments/ball-640-controls-20261122/README.md) · [Basel](docs/experiments/ball-basel-confirmation-20261122/README.md)

**Machine access.** On the joint serve, direct positions localized 10/12 visible balls within ten pixels. One frozen detector found 8/12 on PointStream and 0/12 on either conventional or neural video. On a densely labeled clip at 63.06 versus 63.36 kbps, WASB went from 5/261 to 235/261 and MonoTrack from 7/261 to 229/261. Their absent-frame errors rose from 3 to 11 and 0 to 8 on 30 frames. Direct positions achieved 240/261 and 2/30. Receiver masks also beat segmenting ordinary and neural video on the joint serve (IoU 0.762, 0.312 and 0.638). Ordinary AV1 reached 0.761 near 65 kbps. Both 17-joint pose tracks were readable from the packet in 38 ms on a CPU. [Joint ball](docs/experiments/joint-machine-20261122/README.md) · [Second detector](docs/experiments/second-ball-judge-20261122/README.md) · [Prospective Adelaide test](docs/experiments/ball-adelaide-prospective-20261122/README.md) · [Player masks](docs/paper/figures/player-tasks/starved-masks.pdf)

**Paying for a return.** Over 226.1 seconds, PointStream used 841,055 bytes versus 859,577 for AV1 with identical intervening footage. Twelve sender-defined return samples favored PointStream on both bodies (0.248 vs 0.301 DISTS) and ball (0.285 vs 0.324). Whole-passage luma was 24.45 vs 24.53 dB. Continuous AV1 achieved 26.13 dB at 27.07 kbps. [Indexed passage and verified playback](docs/experiments/cumulative-joint-20261122/README.md)

## Submission decision

Rewrite around scene-level choice, foreground fidelity and direct access to motion. Ball-only reconstruction fit within the tested clip’s playback duration on a laptop. Player generation took about 16 minutes per person for 4.8 seconds of video on a campus GPU. Describe that mode as offline. Confirm the joint result on independently labeled footage from a second match before claiming a general foreground gain. Racket grip remains visibly unreliable.
