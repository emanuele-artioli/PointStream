# Research brief

Can one transmitted representation preserve the action for viewers at constrained rates and expose its motion to a machine? The first question is perceptual and the second asks what an application can read, how accurately, and at what processing cost. A conventional stream, ROI coding, tracked pixels, rendered trajectories and pose-conditioned synthesis are all credible choices. The [paper figure plan](PLAN.md) defines how to choose between them.

## Evidence we should build on

- **Ball display.** At 19.965 kbps, a complete cold-start trajectory stream reduced ball-crop DISTS from 0.4270 to 0.3643 against 20.063 kbps ordinary AV1 on one 50-fps rally. Tracked pixels at 60.670 kbps reduced the same distance from 0.3785 to 0.2933 against matched AV1. Include the larger-resolution plain, ROI and native neural anchors in the rate–quality plot. The older three-match study found 26.28% mean rate savings over its own equal-quality support. The new roughly 61 kbps three-match test includes a 60-fps match on which a cheaper conventional stream scored slightly better. [Rate–quality figures](docs/paper/figures/starved-choice/README.md) · [Three-match results](docs/experiments/ball-confirmation-starved-20261122/README.md)
- **Player display.** Eight paired historical windows from three matches improved equal-match mean player-region DISTS by 0.0689 over DCVC-UF under modeled two-hour appearance and adaptation reuse. On one source-denied receiver replay, the 70/30 player-and-complement DISTS improved from 0.3141 for ordinary AV1 to 0.2522 with generated players. The neural control already reached 0.2543 at 49.778 kbps and beat the joint receiver on weighted LPIPS. The joint receiver cost 469.335 kbps cold and 65.473 kbps with both references already valid. Ordinary AV1 better preserved the background and motion-compensated body changes. [Pooled players](docs/paper/figures/players-starved/README.md) · [Independent regional test](docs/experiments/starved-joint-metric-20261122/README.md) · [Motion](docs/experiments/starved-temporal-20261122/README.md)
- **Machines.** At a 0.486% rate match on another clip, the same RGB detector found the ball on 5/261 visible frames with AV1 and 235/261 with a rendered fitted trajectory. Its absent-frame false detections rose from 3/30 to 11/30. The receiver read coordinates directly on the same delivery and scored 240/261 and 2/30 at a ten-pixel tolerance. The two interfaces differ at five pixels. Direct player masks were similar in mean IoU to ordinary AV1 segmentation on 24 body labels. [Machine ball results](docs/experiments/ball-matched-low-20261122/README.md) · [Player segmentation](docs/paper/figures/player-tasks/README.md)
- **Systems.** Charge both the first player appearance and its actual return mapping. A WebP reference pair cost 242,646 bytes at startup and 354 bytes to reuse on the next observed shot, versus 215,667 bytes to refresh there. One author preferred reuse to refresh for both return tracks and rated it barely acceptable. These are reference-channel bills from an exploratory render. On a separate cutaway passage a conventional AV1 long-term reference reduced complete rate by 4.84% over previous-frame prediction at equal quality. [Appearance reuse](docs/experiments/cross-shot-reference-20261122/README.md) · [Conventional reference control](docs/experiments/native-return-control-20261122/README.md)

## Submission decision

Build the evaluation around the figures that demonstrate a claim on matched streams. Use an object-specific claim when only that object has been confirmed. Report training exposure, reuse, whole-frame and temporal costs in the corresponding evaluation. A separate comparison on 16 official racket boxes found the ball-tiled display worse than the strongest tested ordinary/ROI control in each of three matches. Keep equipment separate from the body claim. [Racket diagnostics](docs/experiments/starved-equipment-20261122/README.md)
